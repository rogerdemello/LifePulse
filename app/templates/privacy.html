Privacy

The short version: LifePulse has no account system, no database, and writes nothing to disk. Your answers exist for the fraction of a second it takes to calculate a result, and then they're gone.

What happens when you submit a form

  1. Your answers are sent to the server over HTTPS.
  2. They're held in memory, turned into numbers, and passed to a model that runs on this server — not a third-party AI service.
  3. The result page is rendered and sent back to you.
  4. The request ends and your answers are discarded. Nothing is written to a file, a database, or a cookie.

What we don't do

  • No accounts, sign-ups, or email addresses.
  • No database. There is nowhere for your answers to be stored, so there is nothing to breach, sell, or hand over.
  • No analytics or advertising trackers.
  • No sending your health answers to any external service. Every model runs locally.
  • No health data in cookies.

Being precise about the exceptions

Two things do leave this server, and you should know about both:

  • The nutrition lookup. When you search for a food, that food's name is sent to the USDA FoodData Central API. Nothing else goes with it — no health answers, no identifier.
  • Fonts, styles and icons load from public CDNs (jsDelivr, Google Fonts, unpkg). Those providers see your IP address and which page you loaded, as they would for any site using them. They never see your answers.

Standard web-server request logs may record IP addresses and the URLs visited. Form contents are sent in the request body and are not written to those logs.

Your results are yours to keep

Because nothing is saved, you can't come back later and find a result — that's the trade for storing nothing. Print or save the page while you have it if you want to bring it to an appointment.

This is a personal project

LifePulse is open source and not a registered medical device or a healthcare provider. It is not covered by HIPAA, GDPR data-controller obligations, or similar frameworks — largely because it holds no personal data to be covered. Treat it as a well-intentioned calculator, not a clinical service.
