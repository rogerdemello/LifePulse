Privacy

The short version: LifePulse has no account system and no database. Nothing you enter is stored on our server — your answers exist there for the fraction of a second it takes to calculate a result, and then they're gone. The one thing that outlives the request is the visit summary, and it is saved in your own browser, never uploaded.

What happens when you submit a form

  1. Your answers are sent to the server over HTTPS.
  2. They're held in memory, turned into numbers, and scored on this server — not by a third-party AI service.
  3. The result page is rendered and sent back to you.
  4. The request ends and your answers are discarded. Nothing is written to a file, a database, or a cookie.

What we don't do

  • No accounts, sign-ups, or email addresses.
  • No database. There is nowhere for your answers to be stored, so there is nothing to breach, sell, or hand over.
  • No analytics or advertising trackers.
  • No sending your health answers to any external service. {% if llm_enabled %} Every model that scores an assessment runs on this server; the one outside service is described below, and it never sees an answer. {% else %} Every model runs locally. {% endif %}
  • No health data in cookies, and nothing stored on our server.
  • No assessment answers or results sent anywhere. The wording on the result pages was written ahead of time and ships with the app, so those pages make no outside request at all.

Being precise about the exceptions

  • The nutrition lookup. When you search for a food, that food's name is sent to the USDA FoodData Central API. Nothing else goes with it — no health answers, no identifier.

  • The “what's bothering you” box. If you type a description on the start page, what you type is sent to Azure OpenAI to work out which check fits. It may ask you up to two follow-up questions, and your answers to those are sent the same way — so it is the sentences you type into that box, not just the first one. Nothing you enter into an assessment goes with it: not the answers, not the results, not the scores. The emergency check runs here first, on this server, before anything is sent, and its result cannot be changed by the model. The exchange is not stored — it travels in the page and is gone when you leave. Azure OpenAI does not train on customer data.
    • {% endif %}
  • Nothing else. Every font, stylesheet, script and icon on this page is served from this site. Your browser makes no request to anyone else while you use LifePulse, so there is no third party to see your IP address or which assessment you opened. This used to say that fonts, styles and icons came from public CDNs, and that those providers saw your address on every page load. That was true, and it sat oddly next to everything above it, so the CDNs were removed rather than the paragraph.

Standard web-server request logs may record IP addresses and the URLs visited. Form contents are sent in the request body and are not written to those logs.

The visit summary is saved — on your device

If you choose Add to visit summary on a result page, that result — including the answers it was based on — is written to your browser's local storage so you can collect several assessments onto one printable page. Being straight about what that means:

  • It stays on this device, in this browser. It is never uploaded, and it is not attached to requests the way a cookie would be — our server never receives it.
  • It persists until you remove it. Use Clear on the summary page, or clear your browser data.
  • Anyone else using this browser profile could open the summary page and read it. On a shared or public computer, clear it when you're done.

If you never use that button, nothing is saved anywhere at all — and you won't be able to come back later and find a result, which is the trade.

This is a personal project

LifePulse is open source and not a registered medical device or a healthcare provider. It is not covered by HIPAA, GDPR data-controller obligations, or similar frameworks — largely because it holds no personal data to be covered. Treat it as a well-intentioned calculator, not a clinical service.
